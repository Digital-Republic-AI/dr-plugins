# Methodologies, Frameworks, Techniques and Approaches for Brainstorming and Disruptive Ideas - Deep Research Summary

> Date: 2026-04-29
> Type: Deep Research
> Goal: Personal application (individual use on one's own projects)
> Knowledge Level: Basic (knows Design Thinking and classic brainstorming)
> Interests: Classic frameworks, disruptive techniques, practical facilitation, corporate innovation

## Executive Summary

The research covered five complementary fronts: the cognitive foundations of creativity, consolidated classic frameworks, explicitly disruptive techniques, corporate innovation frameworks and facilitation practices. The first important finding is that oral group brainstorming, in the form simplified by companies after Alex Osborn (1953), consistently produces fewer and worse ideas than individual ideation followed by consolidation. Three mechanisms explain this empirical failure: production blocking, social loafing and evaluation apprehension. For someone applying it personally, this is good news: most of the most powerful techniques work very well solo, and some only work solo.

The second finding is that there is a clear division of function among frameworks. The classics (Design Thinking, SCAMPER, Six Thinking Hats, TRIZ, Morphological Analysis) serve to generate a structured volume of ideas within an already defined space. The disruptive techniques (inversion, premortem, first principles, biomimicry, design fiction, backcasting) serve to reframe the problem and expand the solution space beyond the obvious. The corporate frameworks (JTBD, Blue Ocean, Lean, Three Horizons) serve to connect ideas to real value, validate at low cost and organize a portfolio of bets. Mixing the three layers is what distinguishes a mature ideator from a chaotic one.

The third finding is that the real work of creativity lies in three undervalued points: how you formulate the problem (a well-built How Might We outperforms 80% of the later effort), how you separate divergence from convergence (combining them kills both), and how you build a routine of continuous capture and incubation. Moderate constraints, REM sleep and intersections between distant domains (the Medici Effect) are proven scientific levers - not creative folklore.

The fourth finding is practical: for someone at a basic level who applies it personally, a lean portfolio of six tools covers 90% of cases: solo brainwriting, How Might We, SCAMPER, Crazy 8s, Premortem and Lean Canvas. The other frameworks are marginal gains that are worth the investment after these six are automated. Avoid the common mistake of collecting techniques without mastering any.

The fifth finding is that disruption rarely comes from "having better ideas" - it comes from applying structurally different questions (inversion, first principles, JTBD, backcasting) to problems that are already mapped. Disruption is methodological, not inspirational.

## Key Findings

### 1. Foundations: the science behind any technique

- **Classic brainstorming does not work as promised.** Meta-analyses and controlled studies (Diehl & Stroebe, Mullen et al.) show that individuals thinking alone generate more ideas, and more original ideas, than groups brainstorming orally. Osborn's 4 rules (defer judgment, quantity over quality, wild ideas, build on others' ideas) are correct; it is the oral group format that fails.
- **Three structural causes of group failure:** (1) production blocking - only one person speaks at a time; (2) social loafing - the presence of the group reduces individual effort; (3) evaluation apprehension - fear of judgment leads to self-censoring ideas.
- **Brainwriting (Rohrbach's 6-3-5) generates 71% more ideas** than oral brainstorming, eliminating all three mechanisms.
- **Guilford's model:** creativity has four measurable dimensions - fluency, flexibility, originality, elaboration. Different techniques optimize different dimensions.
- **Divergence and convergence are opposite cognitive modes** and must not be mixed in the same phase. The Design Council's Double Diamond formalizes this in two sequential cycles.
- **Incubation has a physiological basis.** Studies (PNAS, Science Advances) show that REM sleep and stage N1 (the threshold of sleep) significantly increase creative problem solving. Napping with an object in hand (the Edison/Dalí technique) is scientifically defensible.
- **Moderate constraints increase creativity.** A meta-analysis of 145 studies (HBR 2019) shows a U-shaped curve: neither zero nor maximum constraint - moderate constraint is the optimal point.

### 2. Classic frameworks: the mandatory repertoire

- **Design Thinking (5 phases)** - Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test. Strong for human problems with ambiguity. Legitimate criticism: it can be conservative and generate incremental innovation.
- **Double Diamond** - Discover/Define/Develop/Deliver. More useful than classic DT for reinforcing divergence-convergence discipline.
- **Design Sprint (Jake Knapp/GV)** - 5 days, accelerated decision-making, prototyping and testing with real users. Adaptable for individual use in one week.
- **SCAMPER** (Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to other uses, Eliminate, Reverse) - a checklist of systematic provocations. Excellent for solo ideation in 30-90 minutes, very low learning curve.
- **Six Thinking Hats (De Bono)** - separates modes of thinking (facts, emotion, risks, optimism, creativity, process). Useful solo for structured self-debate.
- **Lateral Thinking (De Bono)** - Po-provocation, Random Entry, Challenge, Reversal. Tools to generate "movement" instead of judgment.
- **TRIZ** - 40 inventive principles derived from the analysis of 200 thousand patents. Resolves technical contradictions. More "engineering-oriented", steeper learning curve, but the only framework with a systematic inventive basis.
- **Zwicky's Morphological Analysis** - breaks a problem down into parameters and variants; generates the complete combinatorial space. Powerful for product/service design problems.
- **Synectics** - four types of analogy (direct, personal, symbolic, fantasy) to make the strange familiar and the familiar strange.

### 3. Disruptive techniques: how to get beyond the obvious

- **Inversion (Munger)** - "how do I guarantee the worst possible outcome?" - reveals traps and counterproductive behaviors that are invisible in direct thinking.
- **Reverse Brainstorming / Worst Possible Idea / Anti-Problem** - variants of inversion applied to ideation. They lower the psychological barrier and produce quickly.
- **Assumption Busting** - list implicit assumptions and challenge them one by one with "what if the opposite were true?". Classic case: Pumpkin Spice Latte (Starbucks discovered that extreme seasonality sold more than consistent flavor).
- **Premortem (Gary Klein)** - imagine that the project failed and list the causes. Generates 30% more identified risks than traditional prospective analysis. Works both for projects and for early-stage ideas.
- **First Principles Thinking** - decompose down to axioms and rebuild. Used by SpaceX to reduce rocket cost by 10x. Applicable individually with reverse 5 Whys.
- **Reframing (4 dimensions)** - change the subject, scale, timeframe or perspective of the problem. Mindtools' Reframing Matrix formalizes it.
- **Biomimicry** - 3.8 billion years of evolutionary R&D as a library of solutions. AskNature.org as a searchable repository. Cases: Velcro (burdock), Shinkansen (kingfisher), Eastgate ventilation (termite mounds).
- **Distant analogy and cross-industry** - F1 taught a pediatric hospital to reduce handoff errors by 42%. Henry Ford took the assembly line from slaughterhouses. Principle: transfer the mechanism, not the artifact.
- **Random Word/Random Image** - forces the brain to create connections between the problem and an arbitrary stimulus. It works through the cognitive "chaotic attractor" principle.
- **Medici Effect (Frans Johansson)** - innovation happens at the intersections of fields. Reducing associative barriers personally requires deliberate reading/exposure outside one's own domain.
- **Scenarios (Pierre Wack/Shell)** - 2x2 matrix of critical uncertainties, narratives per quadrant.
- **Futures Cone (Voros)** - seven categories from the projected to the preposterous; extreme absurdities generate plausible present insights.
- **Backcasting** - start from the desired future and work backward. Identifies bottlenecks where innovation has the most leverage.
- **Design Fiction (Dunne & Raby)** - diegetic artifacts that materialize future worlds. Makes the speculative concrete.
- **Sci-Fi Prototyping (Brian David Johnson/Intel)** - short narratives based on real technology. Used by Nike, Ford, Boeing.
- **STEEP/PESTLE + weak signals** - systematic scanning (Social, Technological, Economic, Environmental, Political) for incipient changes.

### 4. Corporate innovation: connecting ideas to value

- **Jobs-to-be-Done (Christensen)** - the customer "hires" the product to do a job (functional + emotional + social). Milkshake case: it was hired to make the morning commute less tedious, not to be a dessert. Repositioning generated 7x in sales.
- **Switch Interview (Bob Moesta)** - four forces of progress: Push (frustration with the status quo), Pull (attraction to the new solution), Anxiety (fear of the new), Habit (inertia of the old). Innovation happens when push + pull > anxiety + habit.
- **Outcome-Driven Innovation (Tony Ulwick)** - 8-step Job Map + measurable Desired Outcomes + Opportunity Score (importance + dissatisfaction - 10). Score >12 = hot opportunity.
- **Disruption Theory (Christensen)** - low-end disruption (serves an underserved segment with a "good enough" product) vs. new-market disruption (creates a new market among non-consumers). Distinguish from mere sustaining innovation.
- **Blue Ocean (Kim & Mauborgne)** - Value Innovation = differentiation + low cost simultaneously. Strategy Canvas maps competing factors. ERRC Grid (Eliminate, Reduce, Raise, Create) rebuilds the value curve. Cases: Cirque du Soleil, [yellow tail], Nintendo Wii.
- **Six Paths Framework** - six paths to discover blue oceans: alternative industries, strategic groups, buyer chain, complementary offerings, functional/emotional appeal, trends.
- **Three Tiers of Noncustomers** - looking at who does not consume the category reveals a blue ocean; noncustomer demand is usually larger than customer demand.
- **Lean Startup (Eric Ries)** - Build-Measure-Learn loop, MVP, validated learning. Pivot vs. persevere based on metrics, not opinion.
- **Lean Canvas (Ash Maurya)** - 9 blocks: focuses on problem, solution, segment, unique value proposition, unfair advantage, channels, metrics, costs, revenues. More startup-oriented version of the Business Model Canvas.
- **Three Horizons (McKinsey)** - H1 core (extension of today), H2 adjacent (12-36 months), H3 transformational (3+ years). HBR 2019 critique: linear time no longer works; apply it as a space of bets, not a schedule.
- **Innovation Ambition Matrix (Nagji & Tuff)** - axes "where to compete" x "how to compete". Empirical distribution that produces the best return: 70% core / 20% adjacent / 10% transformational, but reverse return (10/20/70).
- **10 Types of Innovation (Doblin/Larry Keeley)** - innovation is not only product. Configuration (profit model, network, structure, process), Offering (performance, system), Experience (service, channel, brand, engagement). Ideas with 5+ types have a higher probability of disruption.
- **Discovery-Driven Planning (Rita McGrath)** - reverse income statement: start from the required return, calculate what needs to be true, turn assumptions into tests.

### 5. Practical facilitation: how to execute (even solo)

- **A well-formulated How Might We** defines the quality of the output. Neither too broad ("how to improve health") nor too narrow ("how to sell more X in Y"). Goldilocks balance.
- **1-2-4-All (Liberating Structures)** - 12 minutes, parallel, scalable. Works solo as 1-2 (me now, me after incubation).
- **Crazy 8s** - 8 ideas in 8 minutes, sketches, radical time constraint forces quantity. Pillar of the Design Sprint.
- **Brainwriting 6-3-5** - 6 people, 3 ideas, 5 minutes, rotation. Solo: 3 sessions spaced throughout the day.
- **25/10 Crowd Sourcing** - fast for prioritizing with a large group.
- **Wicked Questions, Troika Consulting** - structures for complex problems.
- **Convergence:** Dot Voting (25% rule), NUF Test (New, Useful, Feasible - multiplication, not sum), Impact x Effort Matrix, Note-and-Vote.
- **Solo:** mind mapping, freewriting, morning pages, walking + thinking, starbursting, Bullet Journal/Zettelkasten for continuous capture.
- **Critical antipatterns:** HiPPO (the opinion of the highest-paid person wins), groupthink, premature judgment, premature convergence, false consensus, dominant facilitator, social loafing.
- **Digital tools:** Miro, FigJam, Mural - increasingly converging (AI features for clustering and idea generation). Notion for a persistent repository.

## Comparative Analysis

### When to use each framework

| When you need... | Use | Typical time |
|---------------------------|-----|--------------|
| Quick volume of ideas on a known problem | SCAMPER, Crazy 8s, Brainwriting | 30-60 min |
| Deeply understand a human problem | Design Thinking, JTBD, Switch Interview | days to weeks |
| Reframe a stuck problem | First Principles, Reframing Matrix, Inversion | 1-2 hours |
| Get beyond the obvious and generate real disruption | Biomimicry, Random Entry, Design Fiction, Backcasting | 2-4 hours |
| Resolve a technical contradiction | TRIZ (Matrix + 40 principles) | 4-8 hours |
| Map the complete solution space | Zwicky's Morphological Analysis | 2-4 hours |
| Structured solo self-debate | Six Thinking Hats | 60-90 min |
| Identify risks before starting | Premortem | 30-45 min |
| Position a product against the market | Strategy Canvas + ERRC Grid | 2-4 hours |
| Validate an idea with minimal risk | Lean Startup + MVP + Lean Canvas | weeks |
| Structure a portfolio of ideas | Three Horizons, Innovation Ambition Matrix | single session |
| Quantify opportunities | Outcome-Driven Innovation + Opportunity Score | days |

|--------|----------------|----------------------------|

## Recommendations

### Suggested adoption sequence (basic level, personal use)

**Week 1-2: Foundation**

1. Adopt **solo brainwriting** as the default. Replace "I'll sit and think" with "I'll write 20 ideas in 15 minutes without judging".
2. Practice formulating a **How Might We** before every ideation session. No more "I need ideas for X".
3. Implement **continuous capture** - physical notebook, Notion or Apple Notes - with a single rule: record the raw idea before evaluating it.

**Week 3-4: Trigger tools**

4. Internalize **SCAMPER** by memorizing the 7 letters. Apply it to any blocked problem.
5. Add **Crazy 8s** for quick sketches when the problem allows a visual form.
6. Add **Premortem** before any important decision.

**Month 2: Controlled disruption**

7. Learn **Inversion** as second nature. "How do I guarantee this fails?" before "how do I make it work?".
8. Practice **First Principles** on problems where "it has always been this way" blocks progress.
9. Practice **Reframing** with the 4 dimensions (subject, scale, timeframe, perspective).

**Month 3: Connection to value**

10. Study **JTBD** in depth; do a Switch Interview with yourself about a recent decision.
11. Use **Lean Canvas** for any product/service/personal project idea before investing time.

**Month 4-6: Specialization**

12. Choose between **Blue Ocean** (if the focus is strategic differentiation) or **TRIZ** (if the focus is technical resolution) or **Design Fiction** (if the focus is exploring futures).
13. Add **Biomimicry** and **cross-industry analogy** to the search repertoire.
14. Establish a monthly **horizon scanning** routine (STEEP/PESTLE).

### Anti-recommendations (what to avoid)

- **Do not do unstructured oral group brainstorming.** The research is unequivocal: it produces fewer and worse ideas. Use brainwriting.
- **Do not skip problem formulation.** Every hour spent on HMW saves hours in generation.
- **Do not mix divergence and convergence in the same phase.** It is the number one beginner mistake.
- **Do not collect frameworks.** Master six and always use them. The others are marginal gain.
- **Do not ignore incubation.** If the idea did not come in 90 minutes, stop, sleep, walk. Insist little.
- **Do not confuse ideation with innovation.** Ideation without validation (Lean) and without connection to value (JTBD) is catharsis, not innovation.

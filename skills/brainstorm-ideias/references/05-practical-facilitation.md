# Brainstorming and Disruptive Ideas - Practical Facilitation

> Research Date: 2026-04-29
> Angle: Practical facilitation - sessions, concrete exercises, templates, digital tools, anti-patterns and checklists
> Depth: detailed
> Language: en (translated from pt-BR)

## Overview

Facilitating an ideation session goes far beyond gathering people in a room and asking them to think creatively. The quality of the ideas generated depends directly on the structure adopted, the exercises chosen, the level of psychological safety established and the facilitator's skill in balancing divergence and convergence. This document covers the full arsenal of practical facilitation: from session design and technique selection to the anti-patterns that silently sabotage collective creativity.

The material is organized for immediate use both by those who facilitate groups and by those who practice ideation individually. Each technique includes estimated time, required materials and digital variations. The checklists at the end make it possible to prepare and run sessions without improvising at critical moments.

The underlying context is the strict separation between divergence (generating without judging) and convergence (filtering and deciding). Mixing these two phases is the root cause of most failures in brainstorming sessions. The whole structure of this document respects that separation.

## Problem Framing: How to Build an Effective "How Might We"

Before any idea generation technique, the problem must be framed correctly. The "How Might We" (HMW) question is the standard Design Thinking instrument for this. When well formulated, it broadens the solution space without losing focus on the real problem.

### The Three Intentional Words

According to the [Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-might-we-questions/), each word in "How Might We" is deliberate:

- **How**: suggests the problem can be solved, orienting toward action
- **Might**: allows exploring possibilities without premature commitment to a solution
- **We**: acknowledges that the solution is collaborative

### Five Framing Rules

1. **Anchored in real research**: the question must emerge from an observed problem, not from assumption. Avoid "How might we improve the user experience?" without specific evidence.
2. **No embedded solutions**: "How might we send more notifications?" already embeds the solution. The correct form would be "How might we keep the user informed when something changes?"
3. **Calibrated breadth**: neither too generic nor too restrictive. The question should open room for at least 10 distinct ideas.
4. **Focus on the desired outcome**: prefer verbs such as "increase", "create", "ensure" over "reduce" or "avoid".
5. **Positive language**: concentrate on the desired future state, not on eliminating the current problem.

### Table: Weak HMW vs. Strong HMW

| Observed Problem | Weak HMW | Strong HMW |
|---|---|---|
| User does not know which form to fill out | How might we tell the user which form to use? | How might we make the user feel confident that they are filling it out correctly? |
| Return process is confusing | How might we make returns less difficult? | How might we make the return process fast and intuitive? |
| Meetings take too long | How might we reduce meeting time? | How might we ensure that every meeting produces a clear decision? |

Source: [NN/G - How Might We Questions](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-might-we-questions/)

### Process for Creating HMWs in a Group

1. Run research or a problem survey (5-10 min)
2. Each participant writes 3 to 5 HMWs individually (5 min)
3. Share and group similar questions (5 min)
4. Vote on the most promising ones to use as the trigger for the ideation session (3 min)

## Idea Generation Exercises (Divergence Phase)

### 1-2-4-All (Liberating Structures)

One of the most versatile structures for including all participants simultaneously, regardless of group size. Developed by Henri Lipmanowicz and Keith McCandless. Source: [Liberating Structures - 1-2-4-All](https://www.liberatingstructures.com/1-1-2-4-all/).

**Total duration:** 12 minutes

**Materials:** paper, pens, Post-its (optional), bell to mark transitions

**Step by step:**

| Phase | Time | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Individual reflection | 1 min | Each person reflects alone on the trigger question, writing down thoughts |
| Pairs | 2 min | Pairs exchange and expand on the ideas each generated individually |
| Foursomes | 4 min | Two pairs join, share and identify common patterns |
| Whole group | 5 min | Each foursome contributes ONE standout idea (maximum 3-4 contributions) |

**Critical facilitation tip:** forcing individual silence in the first phase is the most important step. Inexperienced facilitators jump straight to group discussion, eliminating the diversity of perspectives that the individual-pair-foursome cycle is designed to protect.

**Digital variation:** use boards with separate zones for each phase in Miro/FigJam, with a visible timer.

### Crazy 8s (Design Sprint)

Rapid sketching technique from the Google Design Sprint that forces the generation of eight distinct ideas in eight minutes, breaking the attachment to the first idea (often the least innovative). Source: [Google Design Sprint Kit - Crazy 8s](https://designsprintkit.withgoogle.com/methodology/phase3-sketch/crazy-8s).

**Duration:** 8 minutes of sketching + 10-15 minutes of presentation

**Materials:** A4 sheet folded into 8 parts, pen or marker

**Setting up the physical template:**
```
Fold an A4 sheet in half 3 consecutive times.
Unfold: results in 8 numbered rectangles.
Each rectangle = 1 idea in 1 minute.
```

**Step by step:**

1. Present the central challenge or the selected HMW (2 min)
2. Hand out folded sheets and markers
3. Start the timer: 1 minute per sketch, 8 sketches in total
4. The facilitator calls out each minute to move on to the next rectangle
5. At the end, each person briefly presents the 8 sketches
6. Vote with sticker dots on the most promising ideas

**Fundamental principle:** sketches do not need to be pretty. Geometric shapes, arrows, text and simple icons are enough. The goal is to communicate the concept, not drawing skill. Participants without a design background should be explicitly reassured before starting.

**Digital version:** use FigJam with the native Crazy 8 template, or create a 2x4 grid in Miro with an embedded timer.

### Brainwriting 6-3-5

Silent idea generation method developed by the German Bernd Rohrbach in the 1960s. Ideal for groups where dominant voices tend to suppress more timid participants. In 30 minutes, it can generate up to 108 ideas. Source: [Zapier - What is brainwriting?](https://zapier.com/blog/brainwriting/) and [Collaboard - 6-3-5 Brainwriting](https://www.collaboard.app/en/blog/6-3-5-brainwriting).

**Standard format:** 6 participants, 3 ideas per round, 5 minutes per round

**Total duration:** 30 minutes (6 rounds of 5 min)

**Idea sheet template:**

```
PROBLEM/CHALLENGE: ___________________________________

     | Idea 1         | Idea 2         | Idea 3         |
-----|----------------|----------------|----------------|
P1   |                |                |                |
-----|----------------|----------------|----------------|
P2   |                |                |                |
-----|----------------|----------------|----------------|
P3   |                |                |                |
-----|----------------|----------------|----------------|
P4   |                |                |                |
-----|----------------|----------------|----------------|
P5   |                |                |                |
-----|----------------|----------------|----------------|
P6   |                |                |                |
```

**Step by step:**

1. The moderator presents the problem and hands out one sheet per participant
2. Each participant writes 3 ideas in the 3 columns of the first row (5 min)
3. At the moderator's signal, everyone passes the sheet to the participant on their right
4. Each participant reads the ideas already written and adds 3 new ideas, and may expand or modify the previous ones (5 min)
5. Repeat for 6 rounds until all rows are filled
6. The moderator collects the sheets and leads a discussion to identify promising ideas

**Adaptation for smaller groups:** with 4 participants, use 4-3-4 (4 people, 3 ideas, 4 minutes per round). The important thing is to keep the relationship number of people = number of rounds.

**Advantage over verbal brainstorming:** eliminates the "production blocking" effect, where only one person speaks at a time while the others forget their ideas as they wait.

### 25/10 Crowd Sourcing (Liberating Structures)

Technique for large groups (12 to over 150 people) that quickly generates and ranks the boldest and most actionable ideas. The name comes from the scoring format: 5 rounds, maximum of 5 points per round = maximum 25 points. Source: [The Liberators - 25/10 Crowd Sourcing](https://medium.com/the-liberators/use-25-10-crowd-sourcing-to-spice-up-your-scrum-events-56fdd127e1dc).

**Duration:** 25-30 minutes

**Materials:** index card per participant, pen, space to walk around

**Step by step:**

1. **Writing the idea (3 min):** each participant writes on the card (front): the boldest idea for the proposed challenge AND the first concrete step to implement it. They raise a hand when finished.

2. **Mill & Pass - Round 1 (2 min):** everyone walks around the room exchanging cards with other people (without reading). At the facilitator's signal, each person evaluates the card they are holding and writes a score from 1 to 5 on the back:
   - 1 = little interest
   - 3 = moderate interest
   - 5 = fully committed to this idea

3. **Repeat Mill & Pass for 4 more rounds.** At the end of 5 rounds, each card has 5 scores on the back.

4. **Adding up points (2 min):** each participant adds up the scores on the card they are holding. Maximum score = 25.

5. **Presentation of the highest scores:** participants holding the highest-scoring cards briefly present the idea. The facilitator displays the top-voted cards in a visible place.

**Tip:** use a sound signal (bell, horn) for transitions between Mill & Pass, because the noise of walking makes verbal commands hard to hear.

**Combined use:** the highest-scoring ideas feed a 1-2-4-All or Open Space Technology session to turn them into action plans.

### Wicked Questions (Liberating Structures)

Structure for revealing and articulating paradoxical tensions that block strategic progress. Especially useful in corporate innovation contexts where contradictory objectives coexist. Developed by Henri Lipmanowicz and Keith McCandless. Source: [Liberating Structures - Wicked Questions](https://www.liberatingstructures.com/4-wicked-questions/).

**Duration:** 25 minutes

**Question template:**
```
"How is it that we are ___ [strategy A] 
and at the same time ___ [strategy B] simultaneously?"
```

**Step by step:**

| Phase | Time | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction to the paradox | 5 min | Facilitator presents the concept and examples of paradoxical tensions |
| Individual and pair generation | 5 min | Each person generates pairs of opposing strategies using the template |
| Group selection | 5 min | Each small group selects the strongest Wicked Question |
| Plenary | 10 min | Whole group identifies the most powerful questions and refines them |

**Examples of Wicked Questions:**
- "How is it that we are integrated and autonomous simultaneously?"
- "How is it that we accelerate innovation and maintain operational quality simultaneously?"
- "How is it that we serve the individual customer and scale to millions simultaneously?"

**Fundamental rule:** both sides of the tension must be expressed in an appreciative way, without blame or hierarchy between them. Wicked Questions are not problems to solve - they are paradoxes to navigate.

### Troika Consulting (Liberating Structures)

Peer-to-peer consulting structure in groups of three people, where each participant alternates between the role of client (the one with the challenge) and consultants (those who generate solutions). Source: [Liberating Structures - Troika Consulting](https://www.liberatingstructures.com/8-troika-consulting/).

**Duration:** 30-40 minutes (10-13 min per round x 3 participants)

**Setup:** trios seated knee to knee, without a table

**Step by step per round:**

1. **Preparation (1 min):** everyone reflects on the challenge they will present when they are the client
2. **Client presents (1-2 min):** describes the challenge and what kind of help they are seeking
3. **Clarifying questions (1-2 min):** consultants ask only questions to understand better. No advice yet.
4. **Client turns their back (4-5 min):** the client sits with their back to the consultants. The two consultants freely generate ideas, suggestions and perspectives - without the client in the conversation.
5. **Client reflects (1-2 min):** the client turns around and shares what was most valuable
6. **Rotation:** the next person takes on the role of client

**Why does the client turn their back?** Removing the client from the consultants' field of vision eliminates the unconscious filter of "this will displease the person asking me for help", generating more honest and creative ideas.

### Silent Brainwriting with Post-its (Individual-Collective Variation)

Hybrid technique that combines silent individual ideation with collective organization. Especially useful as an alternative to classic verbal brainstorming for groups of 4 to 20 people.

**Duration:** 15-25 minutes

**Step by step:**

1. Present the HMW or challenge (2 min)
2. **Silent ideation (5-7 min):** each participant writes ideas on individual Post-its, one idea per Post-it. No talking.
3. **Post on the board (2 min):** everyone simultaneously posts their Post-its on a board or wall
4. **Silent reading (3 min):** everyone reads the others' ideas without commenting
5. **Grouping and discussion (5-10 min):** facilitator leads thematic grouping and opens the floor for expansion

**Golden rule:** each Post-it = one idea. Ideas that need two Post-its are usually combining two different things.

### SCAMPER

Framework of creative questions for transforming something existing into something new. The acronym guides seven lenses of analysis. Developed by Bob Eberle. Source: [IxDF - SCAMPER](https://ixdf.org/literature/article/learn-how-to-use-the-best-ideation-methods-scamper).

| Letter | Action | Trigger Questions |
|---|---|---|
| S - Substitute | Substitute | What can be substituted? Which materials, people or rules can be swapped? |
| C - Combine | Combine | What can be merged? Which two products/processes can become one? |
| A - Adapt | Adapt | What can be adjusted? What from another context can be applied here? |
| M - Modify/Magnify | Modify/Magnify | What can be exaggerated? What can be minimized? Which attribute can be amplified? |
| P - Put to Other Uses | Other uses | Who else could use this? In what other context would this work? |
| E - Eliminate | Eliminate | What can be removed without losing value? What is superfluous? |
| R - Reverse/Rearrange | Reverse/Rearrange | What happens if we reverse the order? What if the roles of user and product were swapped? |

**Practical application:** choose an existing product, service or process and go through each of the 7 lenses, generating at least 2 ideas per letter. In 20 minutes, an individual can generate 14+ concrete variations.

### Reverse Brainstorming

Instead of asking "how do we solve the problem", ask "how do we make sure the problem gets as bad as possible". The answers are then inverted to reveal non-obvious solutions. Source: [SessionLab - Brainstorming Techniques](https://www.sessionlab.com/blog/brainstorming-techniques/).

**Format:**
```
Direct question:   "How might we improve customer retention?"
Reversed question: "How might we ensure that all customers abandon 
                    the product within 30 days?"
```

After generating answers to the reversed question ("make onboarding difficult", "have no support", "send irrelevant emails"), each answer is inverted to obtain positive actions ("simplify onboarding", "offer proactive support", "personalize communications").

**Benefit:** removes the creative block caused by the pressure of "having the right answer", releasing ideas that the self-evaluation filter normally suppresses.

## Idea Selection Exercises (Convergence Phase)

### Dot Voting

Quick technique for prioritizing collectively without extensive debate. Source: [Nielsen Norman Group - Dot Voting](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dot-voting/).

**Point allocation rule:** each participant receives a number of points equivalent to approximately 25% of the total number of available options. With 20 ideas on the board, each person receives 5 points.

**Process:**

1. Display all ideas visually (Post-its on the board, cards, slides)
2. Hand out sticker dots or pens (to mark in digital)
3. **Silent, simultaneous voting** - without discussion or lobbying
4. Tally the votes visually
5. Discuss the results and, if necessary, run a second round with the top 3-5 ideas

**Anti-patterns to avoid in dot voting:**

- **Influenced voting:** participants voting after seeing where the leader placed their points. Solution: simultaneous voting or leaders vote last.
- **Vote fragmentation:** spreading 1 point on each option. Solution: allow accumulating all points on a single idea if the participant wants.
- **Visual groupthink:** everyone voting for the same ideas due to the anchoring effect. Solution: use anonymous digital voting or ask everyone to place their dots at the same time.

**Variations:**

- **Weighted dot voting:** mark the points with numbers (1, 2, 3) to indicate intensity of preference
- **Dot voting by criterion:** use different colors to vote on "potential impact" (blue) and "ease of implementation" (green)

### NUF Test (New, Useful, Feasible)

Structured scoring tool for evaluating ideas on three dimensions. Created by Dave Gray. Especially useful when dot voting produces ties or when selections need to be justified. Source: [LinkedIn - Prioritization Methods in Design Thinking](https://www.linkedin.com/advice/0/what-most-effective-way-prioritize-ideas-design).

**Criteria:**

| Criterion | Question | Scale |
|---|---|---|
| New | Is this idea genuinely different from what already exists? | 1-10 |
| Useful | Does this idea solve a real and relevant problem? | 1-10 |
| Feasible | Can this idea be implemented with the available resources? | 1-10 |

**Scoring:** multiply the three values (do not add). An idea with scores 8, 8, 8 receives 512. An idea with scores 9, 9, 1 receives only 81, revealing the feasibility bottleneck.

**Application template:**

```
Idea: _______________________________________________

Evaluation:
  - New (1-10):       ___
  - Useful (1-10):    ___
  - Feasible (1-10):  ___
  
  Total score (N x U x F): ___
  
  Main constraint identified: _______________
```

**Tip:** doing the evaluation individually before comparing scores in the group avoids mutual influence and reveals divergences of perception that deserve discussion.

### Impact x Effort Matrix

Visual prioritization tool that positions ideas on a two-dimensional grid. Source: [Miro - Impact Effort Matrix](https://miro.com/templates/impact-effort-matrix/) and [Workshop Amigo - Impact Effort Matrix](https://workshopamigo.com/the-impact-effort-matrix-your-workshop-prioritization-superhero/).

**The four quadrants:**

| | **Low Effort** | **High Effort** |
|---|---|---|
| **High Impact** | Quick Wins - execute immediately | Big Bets - plan carefully |
| **Low Impact** | Fill-ins - do if time is left over | Money Pits - avoid |

**Workshop process:**

1. Write each selected idea on a Post-it
2. Draw the matrix on a large board (X axis = effort, Y axis = impact)
3. **Silent individual placement:** each participant places the Post-its assigned to them
4. Discussion about placement divergences
5. Define the next actions for the Quick Wins and a development plan for the Big Bets

**Impact criterion:** can be calibrated to the context. Examples: impact on the user, impact on revenue, impact on cost reduction.

---

### Note-and-Vote

Quick convergence technique in three steps: generate, record visually and vote. Frequently used in the context of Design Sprints to avoid long discussions. Source: [SessionLab - Prioritization](https://www.sessionlab.com/library/prioritization).

**Step by step:**

1. **Note (2-3 min):** each participant writes their favorite ideas individually on Post-its
2. **Expose (2 min):** everyone posts simultaneously - without long presentations
3. **Vote (2-3 min):** each participant receives 1-2 points and votes in silence

**Differentiator:** speed is the value proposition. Note-and-Vote is for when there is decision energy, not for in-depth exploration. Total: under 10 minutes.

## Typical Session Agenda

### Standard Session (90 minutes, 5-12 participants)

| Phase | Duration | Activity | Objective |
|---|---|---|---|
| Check-in and warm-up | 10 min | Creative warm-up question or brief game | Create psychological safety, get out of operational mode |
| Problem framing | 10 min | Presentation of the challenge + formulation of the HMWs | Align everyone on the same problem |
| Divergence 1 | 15 min | Individual brainwriting (silent Post-its) | Generate volume without mutual influence |
| Divergence 2 | 20 min | 1-2-4-All or Crazy 8s | Expand and build on individual ideas |
| Break and board reading | 5 min | Silent reading of everything generated | Absorb the collective work |
| Convergence | 15 min | Dot voting + brief discussion of the top 5 | Reduce 50-100 ideas to 3-5 candidates |
| Prioritization | 10 min | Impact x Effort Matrix or NUF Test | Define which ideas move forward |
| Next steps | 5 min | Define owners, deadlines and prototyping format | Ensure the session generates action |

### Short Session (45 minutes)

| Phase | Duration | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | 5 min | One-word check-in or warm-up question |
| Framing | 5 min | Presentation of the central HMW |
| Divergence | 15 min | Brainwriting 6-3-5 (adapted) or silent Post-its |
| Convergence | 10 min | Note-and-Vote |
| Next steps | 10 min | Definition of actions |

### Warm-up Activities

The warm-up serves to take participants out of operational mode and signal that creativity and unusual ideas are welcome. Source: [SessionLab - Brainstorming Techniques](https://www.sessionlab.com/blog/brainstorming-techniques/).

- **"Draw Toast":** participants sketch, without text, how to make toast. Introduces visual thinking and systems without pressure.
- **Free association question:** "What object did you bring today (in your bag, in your pocket) and how does it relate to our challenge?"
- **"Unusual uses":** list 10 different uses for a paper clip in 2 minutes. Warms up divergent thinking.
- **One-word check-in:** each participant shares how they are arriving at the session in a single word.

## Techniques for Individual Use

Research on brainstorming tends to focus on groups, but well-structured individual ideation often produces superior results in quality by eliminating group biases. Source: [Lucidspark - How to Brainstorm Solo](https://lucid.co/blog/how-to-brainstorm-solo).

### Mind Mapping

Visual exploration technique that uses a center-and-branches structure to expand a topic in a non-linear way.

**Basic process:**

1. Write the central topic in the middle of a blank sheet or digital board
2. Create main branches with the categories or dimensions of the problem
3. Expand each branch with sub-branches of free association
4. Do not edit during generation - just record

**Digital tools:** MindMeister, XMind, Miro (mind map template), FigJam.

**When to use:** to explore an unfamiliar topic, map a complex problem before defining the HMW, or organize ideas generated in other sessions.

---

### Freewriting

Write continuously for 10-15 minutes without editing, without lifting the pen, without a self-criticism filter. Based on Julia Cameron's concept of "Morning Pages". Source: [Grammarly - Individual Brainstorming](https://www.grammarly.com/blog/writing-process/individual-brainstorming/).

**Structured format for ideation:**

```
CHALLENGE: [write the HMW or problem in one line]

[Write freely for 10 minutes without stopping. If you get stuck, 
write "I don't know what to write" until something emerges.
Do not go back to correct. Do not judge what comes out.]

After 10 minutes:
- Underline the three most interesting sentences
- Turn each underlined sentence into an actionable idea
```

**Morning Pages as an ongoing practice:** write 3 handwritten pages right after waking up, before any other activity. It is not directed brainstorming, but the unobstructed stream of consciousness often generates creative connections about open challenges.

---

### Cubing Technique

Examine the challenge from six distinct perspectives, as if it were a cube with six faces. Source: [Lucidspark - How to Brainstorm Solo](https://lucid.co/blog/how-to-brainstorm-solo).

| Face | Perspective | Questions |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Describe | What exactly is it? What are its components? |
| 2 | Compare | What does it resemble? What is different? |
| 3 | Associate | What does it make me think of? What memories does it evoke? |
| 4 | Analyze | How is it made? What parts is it composed of? |
| 5 | Apply | How can it be used? Who would benefit? |
| 6 | Argue | What are the pros? What are the cons? |

**Suggested duration:** 3 minutes per face = 18 minutes in total for a full exploration.

---

### Thinking in Motion (Walking + Thinking)

Low-intensity physical activity (walking, swimming) stimulates divergent thinking by releasing dopamine and reducing activity in the prefrontal cortex (responsible for critical judgment). Source: [Lucidspark - How to Brainstorm Solo](https://lucid.co/blog/how-to-brainstorm-solo).

**Practical protocol:**

1. Define the challenge clearly before heading out
2. Walk for 20-30 minutes without devices (or only with a voice recorder)
3. Record ideas by voice (voice memo) without filtering
4. On returning, transcribe and select the most promising ones

**Important:** avoid activities that require attention (driving, complex sports). The goal is to release unconscious processing, not to focus consciously on the problem.

---

### Starbursting (Exploration by Questions)

Technique that generates questions (not answers) around a central problem, ensuring that all angles have been considered before generating solutions. Source: [SessionLab - Brainstorming Techniques](https://www.sessionlab.com/blog/brainstorming-techniques/).

**Format:**

```
              WHO?
               /
HOW? ---- [IDEA] ---- WHAT?
               \
              WHEN?
               |
             WHERE?
               |
              WHY?
```

**Practical use:** spend 2 minutes generating questions for each of the six dimensions before starting to generate solutions. It often reveals aspects of the problem that had not been considered.

## Common Anti-Patterns in Facilitation

Knowing the anti-patterns is as important as knowing the techniques. Most failures in ideation sessions have an identifiable cause. Sources: [Atlassian - The dangers of groupthink](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/teamwork/groupthink), [Alchemia Learning - Why brainstorming fails](https://alchemlearning.com/brainstorming-tricks/), [Psychology Today - Why Brainstorming Is Worthless](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/progress-notes/202211/why-brainstorming-is-worthless-and-groupthink-is-dangerous).

### 1. HiPPO Effect (Highest Paid Person's Opinion)

**What it is:** when the highest-ranking person in the room speaks first, everyone else tends to converge on their opinion, even if they disagree internally.

**Symptoms:**
- Leader shares their idea right at the start of the session
- Participants formulate their ideas as an extension of the leader's idea
- Few genuinely divergent ideas emerge

**Antidotes:**
- Ask everyone to write individually before anyone speaks
- Ask leaders and seniors to share last
- Use silent brainwriting or anonymous voting
- State the rule explicitly at the start of the session: "no hierarchy during idea generation"

---

### 2. Groupthink

**What it is:** unconscious social pressure to maintain harmony in the group leads members to suppress dissenting perspectives, even when those perspectives would be more valuable. Source: [Facilitator School - Groupthink](https://www.facilitator.school/glossary/groupthink).

**Symptoms:**
- Group reaches consensus too quickly
- Unconventional ideas are dismissed with laughter or silence
- No one questions the core assumptions of the problem

**Antidotes:**
- Explicitly designate a "devil's advocate" for each session
- Separate idea generation phases (silent and individual first)
- Use 1-2-4-All to ensure that all perspectives pass through the individual filter before the group
- Actively ask for radical ideas: "What would be the craziest idea that no one would dare to propose?"

---

### 3. Premature Judgment

**What it is:** participants (and sometimes the facilitator themselves) evaluate ideas during the generation phase, destroying the psychological safety needed for unconventional ideas.

**Symptoms:**
- Immediate comments such as "we already tried that" or "that will never work"
- Participants self-censor before speaking
- Volume of ideas decreases after the first 5 minutes
- Ideas become more and more conventional throughout the session

**Antidotes:**
- Explicitly establish the rule "no judgment during divergence" at the start
- Use silent Post-its, eliminating the verbal channel of immediate judgment
- Use the phrase "yes, and..." as an interaction rule (from theatrical improv): every contribution is built upon, never denied
- Physically or temporally separate the divergence and convergence phases

---

### 4. Premature Convergence

**What it is:** the group closes in on one idea too early, before sufficiently exploring the space of possibilities.

**Symptoms:**
- First "good" idea dominates the discussion
- Participants build on the same idea instead of generating new ones
- Facilitator starts prioritizing before the time allocated for divergence

**Antidotes:**
- Strict timebox for the divergence phase
- Explicitly ban the word "but" during generation
- Define a minimum idea quota before allowing evaluation: "we need at least 30 ideas before voting"
- Use a "round 2" of generation after the first cycle: "now generate ideas that contradict the ones we already have"

---

### 5. False Consensus

**What it is:** participants agree verbally with a decision but disagree internally. The objections emerge after the session, sabotaging implementation.

**Symptoms:**
- Decisions made without an explicit record of the concerns raised
- Silence interpreted as agreement
- Implementation meets unexpected passive resistance

**Antidotes:**
- Before closing any decision, ask: "Does anyone have objections that have not yet been expressed?"
- Use gradient voting instead of yes/no: a scale of 1-5 where 1-2 indicate formal objection
- Document not only the decision made, but also the discarded alternatives and the reasons

---

### 6. Dominant Facilitator

**What it is:** the facilitator confuses their role with that of a participant, introducing their own ideas, guiding the group toward preferred conclusions or solving the group's problems instead of structuring the process.

**Antidotes:**
- Facilitator does not vote (or votes last, only as a tiebreaker)
- Facilitator does not generate ideas during the divergence phase
- Facilitator asks open questions, not statements
- Consider an external facilitator for sessions where the natural facilitator has a direct interest in the outcome

---

### 7. Social Loafing (Creative Free-Riding)

**What it is:** in groups, individuals reduce cognitive effort because they know others will cover the gap. The result is that the idea output of a group of 6 people is often lower than the sum of 6 people working individually.

**Antidotes:**
- Always start with individual ideation before any group phase
- Use brainwriting instead of verbal brainstorming
- Assign individual responsibility: "each person is responsible for generating at least 5 ideas before the session"
- Vary the composition of the groups throughout the session

## Preparation and Facilitation Checklists

### Preparation Checklist (72-48h before)

```
PROBLEM DEFINITION
[ ] Is the central HMW formulated and reviewed?
[ ] Is the problem based on data or real observation (not assumption)?
[ ] Is the scope calibrated: neither too broad nor too narrow?

PARTICIPANTS
[ ] Does the group include diverse perspectives (not only experts on the problem)?
[ ] Group size: between 5 and 12 people for a standard session?
[ ] Were participants informed in advance about the central topic?

SESSION DESIGN
[ ] Are the selected techniques suited to the objective (exploration vs. prioritization)?
[ ] Does the agenda respect the divergence → convergence sequence?
[ ] Is there enough warm-up time (minimum 10 min)?
[ ] Is the total time within 90 minutes (maximum to keep focus)?

MATERIALS
[ ] Post-its (at least 2 colors) prepared?
[ ] Enough markers/pens for everyone?
[ ] Board or wall available for posting materials?
[ ] Timer visible to all participants?
[ ] Templates printed or set up digitally?
```

### Opening Checklist (at the start of the session)

```
[ ] Session objective clearly communicated
[ ] Rules made explicit: no judgment, one idea per Post-it, hierarchy suspended
[ ] Facilitator's role clarified: process, not content
[ ] Warm-up activity carried out
[ ] Central HMW visible to everyone throughout the session
[ ] Documentation mechanism defined (who photographs, who records digitally)
```

### Closing Checklist

```
[ ] Next actions defined with owner and deadline (not "someone will do it")
[ ] Decisions documented including discarded alternatives
[ ] Materials photographed or digitized before closing
[ ] Date and format of the next session defined (if applicable)
[ ] Quick retro: what worked / what to change in the next session (2-3 min)
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Separating divergence from convergence** is the most important rule. Mixing judgment with generation destroys the volume and diversity of ideas.

2. **Always start with the individual.** Whether in a group or solo, starting with silent individual reflection eliminates anchoring biases and ensures genuine contributions from everyone.

3. **The HMW is the most critical design of the session.** A poorly formulated question generates irrelevant ideas regardless of the technique used. Investing 10 minutes in the formulation changes the outcome completely.

4. **Quantity before quality in divergence.** Groups that impose on themselves the filter of "only proposing good ideas" generate fewer ideas and less diversity. The rule is: first, quantity.

5. **Anti-patterns have structural remedies.** HiPPO, groupthink and premature judgment are not problems of individual behavior - they are process design problems. The right structure eliminates them without the need for confrontation.

6. **Digital tools amplify good structures, they do not replace them.** Miro, FigJam and Mural are accelerators when the facilitator knows the methodology. Without structure, they are just expensive whiteboards.

7. **Individual use is underestimated.** Mind mapping, freewriting, SCAMPER and Starbursting in consistent individual use produce higher-quality ideas than poorly facilitated group sessions.

8. **Document the discarded ideas.** The Impact x Effort matrix changes over time. Ideas classified as "low-effort, low-impact" today may be "quick wins" in six months with other resources available.

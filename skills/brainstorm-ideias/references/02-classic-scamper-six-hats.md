# Brainstorm and Disruptive Ideas - Classic Frameworks: SCAMPER, Six Hats and Lateral Thinking

> Research Date: 2026-04-29
> Angle: Classic frameworks - SCAMPER, Six Thinking Hats and lateral thinking techniques (Edward de Bono)
> Depth: detailed
> Goal: Apply personally
> Knowledge Level: Basic

## Overview

This document covers three of the most widely used classic frameworks for stimulating creativity and generating innovative ideas: the SCAMPER method, Edward de Bono's Six Thinking Hats, and the Lateral Thinking techniques also developed by De Bono. Together, these frameworks form a practical and complementary arsenal that can be used individually or in a group to solve problems, improve products and services, and break away from habitual thought patterns.

Unlike purely intuitive approaches, these three methods offer structure: concrete questions to ask, sequences of thought to follow, and specific mental operations to carry out. For those starting to explore structured creativity, they represent the most accessible entry point, since they can be applied immediately with pen and paper, without extensive training.

The common thread among the three is the idea that the human mind tends to follow established patterns, and that external tools --- questions, provocations, "hats" --- work as levers to get out of conventional thinking and access new perspectives.

## SCAMPER

### Origin and Context

SCAMPER was developed by the educator Bob Eberle in 1971 and published in the book *Scamper: Games for Imagination Development*. The technique is based on a checklist created earlier by Alex Faickney Osborn, the same person responsible for formalizing brainstorming. Eberle reorganized Osborn's ideas into a memorable acronym, making the process systematic and easy to apply. ([Toolshero - SCAMPER Technique](https://www.toolshero.com/creativity/scamper-technique-bob-eberle/))

The central premise of SCAMPER is that every new idea is, in fact, a modification of something that already exists. Instead of trying to create from scratch, the method encourages looking at what already exists and asking: "What can I change here?". This makes the process less intimidating and more productive.

### The Seven Letters of SCAMPER

#### S - Substitute

Swap components, materials, processes, people or resources for alternatives.

**Questions to ask:**
- What can I swap in this product, process or situation?
- Who else could do this, or what else could it be done with?
- Are there simpler materials, ingredients or steps that would work?
- Can I change the place, the time or the format?

**Personal example:** You want to improve your productivity when studying. Instead of substituting the study technique, substitute the environment (cafe instead of home) or the medium (audio instead of text).

**Professional example:** A company substitutes human service with chatbots in the first levels of support, reducing costs without losing quality on simple requests. ([Designorate - SCAMPER Guide](https://www.designorate.com/a-guide-to-the-scamper-technique-for-creative-thinking/))

#### C - Combine

Join two or more elements, ideas, products or process steps.

**Questions to ask:**
- What happens if I combine this product or idea with another?
- Can I merge two steps of the process into one?
- Are there technologies or resources that could be integrated?
- Is there synergy between two components that I have not yet explored?

**Personal example:** Combining walking with educational podcasts solves two problems at once: physical exercise and continuous learning.

**Classic example:** The smartphone combined a telephone, digital camera, GPS, computer and music player in a single device, creating a revolutionary product category. ([Designorate - SCAMPER Guide](https://www.designorate.com/a-guide-to-the-scamper-technique-for-creative-thinking/))

#### A - Adapt

Adjust, modify or transfer something from another context to the current problem.

**Questions to ask:**
- What can I copy or adapt from another sector, product or context?
- How does something similar work in another field?
- What can I adjust to improve current results?
- Is there a solution already tested somewhere else that I can bring here?

**Personal example:** Adapting the Pomodoro technique (originally for academic productivity) to manage time in long work meetings.

**Professional example:** Restaurants adapted the pizza delivery model with real-time tracking to other food segments during the pandemic. ([Designorate - SCAMPER Guide](https://www.designorate.com/a-guide-to-the-scamper-technique-for-creative-thinking/))

#### M - Modify, Magnify or Minify

Change scale, attributes, shape, color, pace or any characteristic of the product or process.

**Questions to ask:**
- What happens if I drastically increase or decrease this?
- What would it look like if I changed the shape, color, texture or speed?
- What if I exaggerated a characteristic to see the effect?
- What would change if I reduced it to the essentials?

**Personal example:** When writing a text, first try writing a version with double the word limit (magnify) to release all the ideas, and then cut it in half (minify) to find the essence.

**Professional example:** Social networks magnified the reach of personal communication from dozens of contacts to billions of users, fundamentally transforming the way we relate to each other and consume information. ([IMD - SCAMPER Design Thinking](https://www.imd.org/blog/innovation/scamper-method-design-thinking/))

#### P - Put to Another Use

Find alternative uses for the existing product, process or resource.

**Questions to ask:**
- What else could this be used for?
- Are there markets or audiences we have not yet considered?
- Does the waste or byproduct of this process have value in another context?
- Could another department or person use this differently?

**Personal example:** Your work meeting notes can be reused as the basis for a professional reflection article or post.

**Classic example:** McDonald's realized that its real business was real estate, not just food. By buying land and leasing it to franchisees, it created a much more robust revenue source. ([Cleverism - SCAMPER](https://www.cleverism.com/idea-generation-problem-solving-using-scamper-technique/))

#### E - Eliminate

Remove unnecessary elements, steps, characteristics or components.

**Questions to ask:**
- What can I remove without losing the main function?
- Which steps of the process are really necessary?
- What happens if I simplify as much as possible?
- Is there something we do out of habit that adds no value?

**Personal example:** Eliminating all phone notifications except the essential ones can drastically increase focus and the quality of deep work.

**Professional example:** McDonald's eliminated waiters by creating the self-service system, speeding up service and reducing operating costs. ([Toolshero - SCAMPER Technique](https://www.toolshero.com/creativity/scamper-technique-bob-eberle/))

#### R - Reverse or Rearrange

Invert the order, direction or logic of the process or product.

**Questions to ask:**
- What happens if I invert the sequence?
- What if the process started at the end?
- What changes if I put the customer where the supplier was?
- Can I reorganize the components in a different way?

**Personal example:** Instead of waiting to have all the information before starting to learn something new, start with the practical project and learn as needed (inverted learning, like the "project-based learning" model).

**Classic example:** McDonald's inverted the logic of the traditional restaurant: the customer pays first and eats afterward, speeding up cash flow and service. ([Cleverism - SCAMPER](https://www.cleverism.com/idea-generation-problem-solving-using-scamper-technique/))

### How to Run a SCAMPER Session

1. **Define the object of analysis:** A product, service, process, problem or specific situation.
2. **Go through each letter:** Systematically apply the questions of each dimension. It is not necessary to follow the order of the acronym.
3. **Record everything:** Write down all the answers without filtering. Judgment comes later.
4. **Filter and evaluate:** Classify the ideas as "useful", "partially useful" and "not useful" for this moment.
5. **Deepen the most promising ones:** Choose two or three ideas to explore in more detail.

SCAMPER can be completed individually in 30 to 45 minutes. In a group, sessions of 60 to 90 minutes are usually enough to generate dozens of ideas. ([Big Bang Partnership - SCAMPER](https://bigbangpartnership.co.uk/scamper/))

## Six Thinking Hats

### Origin and the Principle of Parallel Thinking

Edward de Bono published "Six Thinking Hats" in 1985. The method starts from a critical observation: in traditional discussions, people think in different ways at the same time --- one is being emotional, another logical, another critical --- creating unproductive conflict. De Bono's solution was **parallel thinking**: everyone thinks in the same direction, at the same time, using the same "hat". ([De Bono Group - Six Thinking Hats](https://www.debonogroup.com/services/core-programs/six-thinking-hats/))

The hats are metaphors, not fixed personality roles. Anyone can use any hat. The goal is to create a temporary social contract: "now, for five minutes, we are all wearing the green hat, that is, we are all being creative without judgment".

### The Six Hats

#### White Hat - Facts and Information

**Function:** Objective focus on available data, facts and information. No interpretations, no opinions. The white hat asks: "What do we know? What do we need to know?"

**Typical use:** Gathering sales data, surveys, objective feedback, market numbers. Identifying information gaps that need to be filled before deciding.

**Common pitfall:** Confusing opinion with fact. The white hat accepts only what is verifiable.

**Individual example:** Before asking for a raise, list all the facts: your delivery history, market salary benchmarks, quantifiable results of your work.

#### Red Hat - Emotions and Intuition

**Function:** Give legitimate space to feelings, intuitions and emotional reactions. No need to justify. The red hat says: "I feel this will not work" --- and that is valid.

**Typical use:** Capturing the group's gut-feeling reaction to a proposal. Checking the team's emotional engagement. Allowing non-rational concerns to surface without shame.

**Why it matters:** Decisions ignore emotions on their own. The red hat ensures that the emotional dimension is considered in a structured way, not suppressed.

**Individual example:** When evaluating a career change, allow yourself to record how you feel about each option --- enthusiasm, fear, relief, resistance --- without trying to rationalize them yet.

#### Black Hat - Criticism and Risks

**Function:** Identify errors, risks, weaknesses and reasons why something may fail. It is the "devil's advocate" hat, of constructive pessimistic thinking.

**Typical use:** Anticipating obstacles, assessing the risks of a plan, identifying logical inconsistencies, questioning assumptions.

**Attention:** The black hat is the most naturally activated in corporate environments, which tends to smother new ideas before they mature. De Bono recommends using it after the yellow hat, so that the positive points are recorded before the criticism.

**Individual example:** When evaluating an investment, specifically list what can go wrong: market risks, personal risks, worsening scenarios.

#### Yellow Hat - Optimism and Benefits

**Function:** Identify the positive points, the benefits, the values and the reasons why something may work. It is optimistic thinking with logic, not fantasy.

**Typical use:** Building the positive case for an idea before criticizing it. Identifying non-obvious advantages. Maintaining productive optimism even in the face of adversity.

**Complement to the black hat:** The yellow-black pair forms the complete assessment: first the best scenario, then the worst, to arrive at a balanced view.

**Individual example:** When receiving critical feedback, force yourself to list three reasons why that feedback may be valid and useful for your growth before reacting to it.

#### Green Hat - Creativity and Possibilities

**Function:** Generation of ideas, alternatives, creative solutions and lateral thinking. In this hat, there is no judgment --- every idea is welcome.

**Typical use:** Generating options when facing a problem, exploring unconventional alternatives, proposing changes, imagining future scenarios.

**Relationship with lateral thinking:** The green hat is the space for lateral thinking techniques. Provocations, random entries and reversals are welcome here.

**Individual example:** Before accepting the first solution that comes up for a problem, spend five minutes in the "green hat" and force yourself to generate at least five completely different alternatives.

#### Blue Hat - Process and Organization

**Function:** Manage the thinking process. It sets the agenda, the sequence of hats, the time of each stage and synthesizes the conclusions. It is the "meta-hat" --- it thinks about how we are thinking.

**Typical use:** Opening and closing of any session. Defining which hat to use at each moment. Summarizing what was decided.

**Fundamental rule:** Every session begins and ends with the blue hat. At the opening, it defines the objective and the sequence. At the closing, it synthesizes the conclusions and defines the next steps. ([Atlassian - Six Thinking Hats](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/productivity/six-thinking-hats))

**Individual example:** When planning a week, start with the blue hat: "What is my main objective this week? What decisions do I need to make? What kind of thinking do I need for each one?"

### Recommended Sequences

The sequence of the hats is not fixed --- it should be adapted to the objective of the session. Some proven sequences:

| Session Objective | Suggested Sequence |
|---|---|
| Problem solving | Blue > White > Green > Red > Yellow > Black > Blue |
| Evaluating options | Blue > White > Green > Yellow > Black > Red > Blue |
| Strategic planning | Blue > Yellow > Black > White > Green > Blue |
| Process review | Blue > White > Yellow > Black > Green > Red > Blue |

**Practical rule:** The black hat should never come first after the white. New ideas need room to exist (green or yellow hat) before being criticized (black hat). ([SessionLab - Six Thinking Hats](https://www.sessionlab.com/methods/the-six-thinking-hats))

### Individual Use vs. Group Use

**In a group:** Everyone uses the same hat at the same time --- this is the essence of parallel thinking. A facilitator (usually wearing the blue hat) guides the transition between hats. Personal conflict is reduced because no one is "being" the critic or the optimist --- everyone is temporarily adopting that perspective. ([Atlassian - Six Thinking Hats](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/productivity/six-thinking-hats))

**Individually:** Go through the hats in sequence and write down your reflections for each one. It is particularly useful for:
- Preparing for an important decision
- Evaluating a proposal before presenting it
- Reviewing a plan before executing it
- Reflecting on an interpersonal conflict before approaching the other party

A complete individual session can take 20 to 40 minutes. It is not necessary to use all the hats every time --- choose the most relevant ones for the context.

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Assigning hats as fixed personalities:** "You always wear the black hat" invalidates the method. The hats are temporary.
- **Switching hats too quickly:** Each hat needs time to be explored in depth.
- **Using the black hat too early:** It kills ideas before they mature.
- **Skipping the blue hat:** Without an opening and closing structure, the session loses focus and does not generate clear conclusions. ([Toolshero - Six Thinking Hats](https://www.toolshero.com/decision-making/six-thinking-hats-de-bono/))

## Edward de Bono's Lateral Thinking

### What Is Lateral Thinking

Edward de Bono coined the term "lateral thinking" in 1967. The central idea is that vertical thinking --- logical, sequential, based on established premises --- is insufficient to solve complex problems or generate real innovation. Lateral thinking moves "sideways", challenging premises and exploring paths that linear reasoning would not visit. ([Wikipedia - Lateral Thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateral_thinking))

The fundamental distinction is between **movement** and **judgment**. Conventional thinking evaluates each idea immediately ("this will not work", "that is impossible"). Lateral thinking uses ideas as stepping stones --- it moves through them to get somewhere new, without stopping to judge along the way. A wrong idea can be the step needed for the right idea.

### Po Technique --- Provocation

#### What It Is

"Po" is a word created by De Bono as a linguistic operator that signals an intentional provocation. It comes from "Provocative Operation". When you use "Po", you are saying: "What follows does not need to be true or logical --- it is a stepping stone to new ideas." ([Wikipedia - Po Lateral Thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_(lateral_thinking)))

#### How to Use It

Build a deliberately impossible, absurd or common-sense-defying statement about your problem. Use the prefix "Po:" to signal that it is a provocation. Then, instead of rejecting the idea, ask: "What does this suggest? What useful principle is embedded here?"

**Classic De Bono example:** Problem --- river pollution by factories.
- Provocation: "Po: the factory is downstream of itself."
- Exploration: If the factory had to draw water from a point downstream of its own discharge, it would be forced to treat its own effluents before discharging them.
- Result: This principle became legislation in some countries. ([Wikipedia - Lateral Thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateral_thinking))

**Personal example:** Problem --- difficulty waking up early.
- Provocation: "Po: the alarm rings the night before."
- Exploration: What can I prepare the night before so that the morning is automatically easier? Clothes laid out, coffee on a timer, agenda reviewed?

#### Techniques for Generating Po Provocations

- **Exaggeration:** "Po: the product costs zero."
- **Reversal:** "Po: the customer pays after 12 months of satisfaction."
- **Negation:** "Po: the meeting has no agenda."
- **Distortion:** "Po: the doctor gets sick along with the patient."
- **Wishful thinking:** "Po: the car drives itself." (This became reality.)

### Random Entry Technique

#### What It Is

The thinker picks an object, image or word at random --- from a dictionary, from a window, from a deck of cards --- and forces associations between that element and the problem they are trying to solve. The goal is not logic, but pattern disruption. ([Wikipedia - Lateral Thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateral_thinking))

#### How to Use It

1. Define the problem or the area of innovation.
2. Open a dictionary to a random page and point to a word, or look out the window and choose the first object you see.
3. List the characteristics and associations of that random element.
4. Force connections between those characteristics and your problem.

**De Bono example:** Problem --- how to improve an office photocopier.
- Random word: "nose."
- Associations: smell, signage, alert, sensitivity.
- Forced connection: the photocopier could emit a lavender scent when paper was running out, turning a problem (lack of paper) into a pleasant sensory experience. ([Wikipedia - Lateral Thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateral_thinking))

**Personal example:** Problem --- how to make your exercise routine more consistent.
- Random word chosen from a dictionary: "passport."
- Associations: travel, identity, permission, new places.
- Forced connection: create a "workout passport" where each session is a "stamp" in a new "city" (muscle or activity), turning the routine into a personal geographic exploration.

### Challenge Technique

#### What It Is

The challenge is not a criticism --- it is a non-threatening question that asks why something exists or works the way it does. Even when something works well, the challenge asks: "Why do we do it this way? Is there a real reason or is it just habit?"

#### How to Use It

Choose any element of the problem or process and systematically ask:
- "Why does this exist?"
- "Why is it done this way?"
- "What would happen if it did not exist?"
- "Are there other ways to achieve the same goal?"

**De Bono example:** Challenging the handle of the coffee cup.
- "Why does the handle exist?" So that people can hold the cup without getting burned.
- Alternatives generated: insulate the whole cup with thermal material, serve at a lower temperature, use materials that conduct less heat. ([Peak Performance Center - Lateral Thinking Techniques](https://thepeakperformancecenter.com/educational-learning/thinking/types-of-thinking-2/lateral-thinking/lateral-thinking-techniques/))

**Personal example:** Challenging the weekly team meeting.
- "Why does it exist?" To synchronize the team and resolve pending items.
- "Why does it last an hour?" Habit, not necessity.
- Alternatives: 15-minute daily, asynchronous document, meeting only when there is a decision to be made.

### Perceptual Change Technique

#### What It Is

Reframing the problem by seeing it from radically different perspectives --- from the customer's point of view, the competitor's, a child's, someone from another sector, or someone who has never heard of the problem before.

**Practical example:** Uber realized that the passengers' problem was not the waiting time itself, but the uncertainty about when the car would arrive. The solution was not to speed up the car, but to show the car on the map in real time --- a perceptual change that transformed the experience without changing the actual waiting time. ([Medium - Five Lateral Thinking Techniques](https://medium.com/@incyweb/five-lateral-thinking-techniques-4467d777bfc6))

### Movement vs. Judgment

This is the most important and most counterintuitive concept of lateral thinking. Instead of judging an idea as good or bad, use it as a movement --- an intermediate step to reach better ideas.

**Movement tools according to De Bono:**
- **Extract the principle:** "The useful principle of this impossible idea is..." and then apply that principle in an achievable way.
- **Focus on the difference:** "What is different here compared to the usual?" and explore that difference.
- **Look for the positive:** Even in a clearly inadequate idea, "what is good here?" can reveal a usable element.
- **Special circumstance:** "In what circumstance would this absurd idea work?" can reveal a valid niche or context. ([Wikipedia - Lateral Thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateral_thinking))

## PMI --- Plus, Minus, Interesting (Positive, Negative, Interesting)

### What It Is and Why It Exists

PMI is an evaluation tool created by De Bono, simpler than the Six Hats, designed specifically to counter the human tendency to judge an idea as good or bad based on first impression. By forcing the systematic exploration of three dimensions --- Plus (P), Minus (M) and Interesting (I) --- PMI expands thinking before the final judgment. ([GroupMap - PMI](https://www.groupmap.com/portfolio/pmi-plus-minus-interesting/))

### The Three Dimensions

**P - Plus:** All the benefits, advantages, strengths and reasons why the idea may work. Not only the obvious ones --- you are forced to go beyond the immediate.

**M - Minus:** All the problems, risks, disadvantages and reasons why the idea may fail. Likewise, go beyond the obvious.

**I - Interesting:** Points that are neither good nor bad, but deserve attention --- unanticipated implications, curious side effects, questions the idea raises, possibilities it opens even if it is not adopted.

### How to Use It Individually

1. Write the idea or proposal at the top of a sheet.
2. Divide the sheet into three columns: P, M, I.
3. Spend two to five minutes on each column, without jumping between them.
4. At the end, decide based on the complete view --- not on first impression.

**Example:** Idea evaluated: "Working from home permanently."

| Plus (P) | Minus (M) | Interesting (I) |
|---|---|---|
| Eliminates commuting | Social isolation | How does this change my relationship with the city? |
| More schedule autonomy | Difficulty switching off | What does the physical office still make sense for? |
| Reduction in personal costs | Domestic interruptions | What new routines would emerge? |
| More time for family | Career advancement may be limited | How does my productivity compare in the long run? |

PMI is particularly useful before a difficult conversation or an important decision, since it ensures you have come into contact with all perspectives before acting. ([De Bono - PMI Lesson Plan](https://www.debono.com/de-bono-thinking-lessons-1/1.-pmi-lesson-plan))

## When to Use Each Tool

The following table guides the choice of framework according to the context:

| Situation | Recommended Tool | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Improving an existing product or service | SCAMPER | Structured checklist focused on modifications |
| Making a complex decision in a group | Six Hats | Eliminates conflict and ensures all perspectives |
| Breaking out of an entrenched thought pattern | Lateral Thinking (Po, Random Entry) | Provokes a break in premises |
| Evaluating an idea before deciding | PMI | Balanced evaluation in three dimensions |
| Solving a problem alone and quickly | SCAMPER or PMI | Can be applied in 30 minutes without a facilitator |
| Preparing an important personal decision | Six Hats (individual) | Forces perspectives that you would naturally avoid |
| Innovating on a product without a clear reference | Random Entry | Breaks with obvious analogies through external stimulus |
| Questioning established processes | Challenge (Lateral Thinking) | Asks "why" in a non-threatening way |

**Effective combinations:**
- **SCAMPER + PMI:** Use SCAMPER to generate ideas and PMI to evaluate the most promising ones before presenting.
- **Lateral Thinking + Six Hats:** Use provocations and random entry in the green hat to generate ideas, then go through the other hats to evaluate.
- **SCAMPER + Lateral Thinking:** When SCAMPER gets stuck on a letter, use Po or Random Entry to unblock. ([Very Big Brain - Creative Tools](https://verybigbrain.com/creativity/scamper-six-thinking-hats-and-other-weirdly-effective-creative-tools/))

## Key Points

1. **SCAMPER is the most accessible framework for beginners:** Seven structured questions that can be applied to any product, process or problem in less than an hour, alone or in a group.

2. **The Six Hats eliminate the "who is right" conflict:** By separating types of thinking in sequence, everyone contributes constructively without personal identities being attacked or defended.

3. **The black hat is the most natural and the most dangerous:** In corporate environments, criticism dominates. Always use it after the green and yellow hats, never before.

4. **Lateral Thinking is not free creativity --- it is discipline:** Po, Random Entry and Challenge are specific mental operations. Learning them and practicing them regularly develops what De Bono calls "creative fluency".

5. **"Movement" is more productive than "judgment" in idea generation:** Resist the impulse to evaluate each idea immediately. Use impractical ideas as stepping stones to achievable ideas.

6. **PMI is the transition tool between generation and decision:** After any creative session, use PMI to evaluate the generated ideas before selecting one.

7. **The combination of the three frameworks forms a complete cycle:** Lateral Thinking to generate disruptive ideas, SCAMPER to develop and modify existing ideas, and Six Hats to evaluate and decide with multiple perspectives.
